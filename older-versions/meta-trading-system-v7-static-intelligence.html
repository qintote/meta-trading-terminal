    What actually moves gold

    • DXY↑ dollar → ↓ gold — the default relationship. When both rise together, flow is driving it: expect wide candles and give the trade room or skip it.
    • Real yields↑ yields → ↓ gold — gold pays no coupon. US 10Y TIPS is the cleanest read.
    • Risk-offFear → bid — geopolitics and equity stress. These moves trend hard and punish mean reversion.
    • Central banksSteady bid — slow structural demand. It shows up as dips being bought, not as intraday signals.
    • PositioningSharp reversals after a vertical week are usually crowded longs being flushed. That is sweep territory.

    News protocol · non-negotiable

    • Flat 30 minutes before a tier-1 printCPI, PCE, NFP, FOMC statement and press conference, Powell testimony.
    • No new entries for 15 minutes afterThe first move is usually a fake. Let the spread come back in first.
    • Never hold full size into FOMCIf you must hold, take half off and accept the gap risk on the rest.
    • Never trade the rollover hour00:00–01:00 GST. Thin book, wide spread, meaningless wicks.
    • Tier-2 data is tradeable with a wider stopJobless claims, PMI, retail sales. Reduce size by half instead of standing aside.

    Gold has no earnings, no coupon and no central bank of its own. It is priced entirely against the opportunity cost of holding it — the real yield — and against the currency it is quoted in. Every macro headline that matters is really a statement about one of those two. That is the whole model; everything below is detail.

    Rates regime — the textbook

    • Real yields lead, the dollar confirms, gold follows inversely
    • Data days produce the range; quiet days mean-revertLevels hold. Sweeps and ranges both work.
    • Tell: gold and DXY move opposite on most days, and yield moves explain the size

    Reserve regime — price-insensitive buying

    • Official-sector buying absorbs supply regardless of price, so dips stop where models say they should not
    • Gold can rise with the dollar for days at a timeFading strength here is the classic way to lose four in a row.
    • Tell: shallow pullbacks, higher lows, and no reaction to hawkish data

    Fear regime — risk-off bid

    • Gold trends and ignores yields entirely
    • Trend following only. Ranges break, sweeps run further than the model allows
    • Tell: gold, the dollar and bonds all bid at once while equities fall

    Liquidation — everything sold

    • A margin event forces gold to be sold because it is the liquid asset, not because the story changed
    • Do not trade it. Spreads gap, stops slip, structure means nothing
    • Tell: gold, equities and bonds all falling together on the same session

    Geopolitics — the only driver that creates trends rather than moves

    • The escalation curve. Gold prices the risk of escalation, not the event. The biggest move comes on the first credible threat, not on the strike itselfBy the time it is confirmed on every screen, the move is priced.
    • Buy the rumour, sell the fact applies fully. Peace talks, ceasefires and de-escalation headlines unwind the premium faster than it went on
    • Duration decides the shape. A one-day headline gives a spike that fully retraces. A structural conflict — supply routes, sanctions, reserve seizures — reprices gold for months
    • Sanctions and reserve freezes are the special case. They do not scare investors, they change what central banks hold. That is the mechanism behind the whole post-2022 bid
    • Trading rule one: never fade day one of a geopolitical trend. Structure breaks, ranges fail, stops get run twice
    • Trading rule two: the retracement comes when the headline stops developing. No new news for six to twelve hours is the signal, not a candle pattern
    • Trading rule three: gaps. Weekend escalation opens gold higher on Monday in Asia — the gap fills far more often than it extends, but only once headlines cool
    • Never hold a leveraged position over a weekend when something is actively escalatingGaps skip your stop; the loss is not the one you sized for.

    Who is on the other side

    • Central banks. Price-insensitive, mandate-driven, buying on a multi-year schedule. They accumulate into weakness, which is why deep sell-offs stallThey are why a bear case in gold rarely turns into a collapse.
    • Macro funds. Trade the rate path. They are the marginal seller when real yields rise and the marginal buyer when cuts get priced
    • ETF investors. Slow, trend-following, momentum-reinforcing. Flows follow price rather than lead it — useful as confirmation, useless as a signal
    • Commercial hedgers and refiners. Sell into strength as a business, not a view. Their offers are what makes rallies grind rather than run
    • Retail and CFD flow. Concentrated in obvious levels and round numbers. That concentration is the liquidity your sweep playbook targets

    How large players actually execute

    • They work orders, they do not take the market. Size is sliced over hours via VWAP and TWAP algos — this is why a real institutional bid looks like repeated absorption at one level rather than one big candle
    • They accumulate against liquidity. Filling a large buy requires sellers, and sellers cluster below obvious lows. That is the mechanical reason a stop raid precedes a real move
    • They hedge with options. Dealer hedging around large strikes pins price near round numbers into expiry, then releases it afterwards
    • They trade the benchmark. The London fixes concentrate volume — expect defended prices and abrupt reversals around them
    • They rebalance on the calendar. Month, quarter and index events produce flow with no narrative attached
    • They are not hunting your stop personally. They are filling size where the liquidity is, and your stop happens to be parked there

    Reading the crowd

    • COT positioning. Managed money net long at an extreme means the buyers are already in — rallies get thinner, and washouts get violent. It is a condition, never a timing tool
    • ETF flows. Record inflows near highs mark crowded trades. Record outflows into strength usually mean the official sector is doing the buying
    • Retail sentiment. Overwhelmingly one-sided retail positioning is a mild contrarian signal, mostly because it marks where the stops are
    • Options skew and open interest. Where the big strikes sit tells you where price will be defended and where it accelerates once through
    • The tell that matters more than any of them: how price responds to news that should hurt it. A hawkish print that fails to break the low is a bid you can trade with

    Consensus versus what to do

    • Everyone bullish, price stalling → distribution. Trade the sweep of the highs, not the breakout
    • Everyone bearish, price refusing new lows → accumulation. The reclaim is the entry
    • A consensus forecast — five thousand dollars, or a crash — is a story about months. It tells you nothing about the next four hoursUse it for bias, never for entries.
    • Never take a position because an institution published a target. You do not have their time horizon, their size, or their hedge

    How gold behaves

    Instrument character — the habits that repeat regardless of the macro story

    Daily rhythm

    • Asia builds a narrow range on thin volume. That range is tomorrow's liquidityRoughly 04:00–11:00 GST.
    • London opens by taking one side of the Asia range, then decides the day's direction
    • New York either extends the London move or reverses it outright — the overlap holds the day's true high or low most of the time
    • After the London close the move is usually done. Late trends are thin and late ranges are traps

    Volatility signature

    • Gold trends harder than it ranges, and reverses faster than most instruments — it is a fear asset with leverage on top
    • Volatility clusters: a wide day is usually followed by another wide day, and a compressed day precedes expansion
    • It respects round hundreds and fifties because that is where retail and options interest sit
    • Wicks are long and frequent. Stops placed at the obvious level get taken by design — always place beyond the wick, never at it

    Structural quirks

    • Spread widens brutally at rollover and on data. Size for it or stand aside
    • The London fixes concentrate volume and often mark a session extreme
    • Weekend gaps are real — headlines do not wait for the open
    • Options expiry pins price near large strikes, then releases it the next session

    What this means for your playbooks

    • Sweeps work because the liquidity is genuinely concentrated and genuinely needed to fill size
    • Trend following works best after the London open and into the overlap, never late
    • Mean reversion only works on quiet, news-free days inside a qualified range — which is fewer days than it feels like
    • Never assume gold will behave like a currency pair. The tails are fatter and the reversals are faster

    Trading the print

    • Flat 30 minutes before, no new entry for 15 afterTRIGGER · the clock, not the chart
    • The first candle is a liquidity event. Both sides get swept before direction appears
    • The move that holds usually starts three to fifteen minutes in, once yields and the dollar have settled
    • That post-print sweep and reversal is your highest-quality setup of the day — but only with the HTF bias, never against it
    • Never widen a stop because the spread widened. Size for it beforehand instead

    Where this stands as of mid-2026

    • The inverse link to real yields dominated for two decades, then broke down through 2024 and 2025 as emerging-market central banks replaced Western ETFs as the marginal buyer — gold made record highs with real yields positive
    • Through 2026 the rates channel has reasserted itself under a more hawkish Fed; J.P. Morgan's July 2026 work put the sensitivity at roughly twenty dollars an ounce per basis point on the ten-year real yield
    • Official-sector demand has continued at historically high levels, which is why sell-offs have found a floor rather than becoming trends
    • Practical reading: both channels are live. Rates set the daily range, the reserve bid sets the floorRe-check this yearly. The marginal buyer changes, and when it does, so does everything above.

    Reading the calendar

    releases → probabilities

    Gold has no yield, no earnings and no cash flow. That single fact drives everything here: its price is set by what you give up to hold it, plus what you will pay for insurance. Every release is only tradeable through those two things.

    1 · Real yields — dominant, inverse

    Real yield is the nominal yield minus expected inflation. When it rises, holding gold costs more and gold falls. This explains the most counterintuitive fact in the market: hot inflation is usually bearish for gold, because a hot print raises the expected policy rate faster than it raises expected inflation, so the real yield rises.

    28 Aug 2026, Jackson Hole: nothing about actual inflation changed, but expected nominal yields jumped and gold fell 2.75% intraday to about $4,474.

    2 · Dollar — amplifier, inverse

    Gold is priced in dollars, so a stronger dollar makes it dearer for every non-dollar buyer. This channel usually points the same way as real yields and amplifies them.

    When it decouples, pay attention. On 14 Aug 2026 yields rose yet gold closed +0.59% because the dollar broke down. Gold following the dollar against yields means positioning is doing the work, not fundamentals.

    3 · Risk premium — independent, positive

    War, fiscal stress, central bank accumulation, de-dollarisation. This channel ignores the calendar and does not mean-revert on schedule.

    In August 2026 gold gained roughly 10% while September hike odds went from 40% to 65%. Channels 1 and 2 were both screaming sell and channel 3 outweighed them.

    The rule that follows. Never short gold on rate expectations alone while a structural bid is active. Channel 3 does not care what the Fed is debating.

    The reaction equation

    If any term is near zero, gold barely moves. This is why a 3× NFP beat produced a shrug on 4 Sep 2026 — the surprise was large but the sensitivity was low; why CPI at a cycle-high 4.2% on 10 Jun 2026 did almost nothing — it came in exactly in line, so the surprise was zero; and why Fed minutes on 8 Jul 2026 moved gold to $4,075 on a small surprise — nobody had priced a 9-8 split, so the room was enormous.

    Practical consequence. In a hike-or-hold regime the downside tail is fatter than the upside, because a hike is a live outcome the market has not fully priced and a cut is not on the table at all.       Price action

      Where price comes from, what a candle is evidence of, and a repeatable way to read a chart. Auction first, order book second, patterns last — in that order, because the patterns only mean something once you know what produced them.

      01

      The auction

      why price moves at all

      A market is a continuous two-sided auction. Price is not a value — it is an advertisement, moved until it finds trade. Everything on your chart is the record of that search.

      Price rises for one reason: buyers are willing to pay the offer, and the resting offers are being consumed faster than they are replaced. It falls for the mirror reason. Nothing else moves it. Not the news, not the indicator, not the pattern — those only change the willingness of participants to lift offers or hit bids.

      An auction has two states, and every read starts by naming which one you are in.

      Balance

      Both sides agree on price and disagree on value, so trade rotates around a centre. Volume builds in the middle, thins at the edges. Time passes and price does not travel. This is where mean reversion works and trend following bleeds.

      Visible as: overlapping candles, repeated rejection of the same two prices, shrinking range.

      Imbalance

      One side is initiating and the other is retreating. Price travels quickly and spends little time at any level, because there is nothing to trade against. This is where trend following works and fading gets run over.

      Visible as: long bodies in one direction, small counter-candles, gaps in the middle of the move.

      The transition

      Balance ends when one side stops defending an edge. Imbalance ends when the initiating side runs out of willing counterparties and price stalls into a new balance. Most of your money is made in the first two hours of an imbalance and lost in the last.

      Visible as: a decisive close beyond an edge that held twice, or an impulse leg that arrives and goes nowhere.

      The five ideas that do all the work

      • ValueThe band where most trade happened. Not the midpoint of the range — the price where the two sides did the most business. Price returns to it far more often than it leaves it behind.
      • ExcessA sharp rejection at an extreme: a long wick, a fast reversal. It is the auction saying "too high" or "too low" and it marks the end of a leg, not a pause in one.
      • Initiative activitySomeone buying above value or selling below it. They are paying for immediacy, which means they have a reason. Initiative moves continue more often than they reverse.
      • Responsive activitySomeone selling above value or buying below it. They are fading a stretched price. Responsive moves fail the moment the initiator has size behind them.
      • RotationPrice travelling from one edge to the other without conviction. A rotating market pays the patient and punishes everyone who mistakes movement for direction.
      Read this first, every session. Is the market balanced or imbalanced, and where is value relative to price? Every other question you ask about a chart is downstream of that one, and getting it wrong makes every pattern you find point the wrong way.
      02

      The order book

      what actually sits under a candle

      Every candle is a compression of thousands of decisions in a queue. You cannot see the queue on spot gold, but you can see its shadow, and the shadow is enough.

      There are only two kinds of order. A limit order rests in the book and provides liquidity — it waits, and it moves nothing. A market order takes liquidity — it consumes resting orders and it is the only thing that moves price. Every move you have ever traded was market orders eating the book.

      • DepthHow much size rests at each price. Thick depth absorbs aggression and price stalls. Thin depth means a modest order travels a long way — which is why the same volume produces a shrug at midday and a vertical leg at the New York open.
      • AbsorptionAggressive orders arriving continuously and price not moving. Someone is filling them all. When absorption ends, price usually goes the other way, hard, because the aggressors are trapped.
      • ExhaustionThe opposite: aggression dries up and price stops advancing even though nothing is defending it. The move ends not because it was pushed back, but because nobody is left to buy.
      • Stops as liquidityA stop is a market order waiting for a trigger price. A cluster of them is a pool of guaranteed fills. Large participants need those fills to get size done, which is why price so often trades to the obvious level and immediately turns.
      • Icebergs and spoofsSize that is hidden, and size that is shown but never intended to trade. Both mean the visible book lies. This is precisely why we read the outcome — the candle — rather than the advertisement.

      Three chart events, explained by the book

      The gap and the fast leg

      Displacement is not strength of conviction. It is thin depth. A leg that travels twenty dollars in four minutes did so because there was almost nothing resting in the way — and the empty space it leaves behind is the fair value gap you later trade back into.

      The wick through a level

      Price trades to where the stops are, the stops fire as market orders, a resting participant fills against them, and price returns inside. The wick is the fill. That is the entire mechanic of the liquidity sweep.

      The level that keeps holding

      Repeated tests with no progress mean someone is refilling the book at that price. It holds until it does not, and when it breaks, it breaks quickly, because the wall that was absorbing everything has been pulled.

      The practical consequence. Stop asking "is this level support?" and start asking "who is filling here, and what happens when they stop?" A level is not a wall. It is a place where someone was willing to trade, until they were not.
      03

      Candles as evidence

      what a bar can and cannot tell you

      A candle is four numbers and a duration. Treat it as evidence of an auction, not as a signal, and it becomes reliable. Treat it as a signal on its own and it will take your money in every market condition.

      • The bodyWhere business got done. A long body means one side dominated for the whole period. A small body means the period ended roughly where it began — disagreement, not direction.
      • The wicksWhere price was rejected. A long wick is an auction that went looking for trade, found none it liked, and came back. It is the most honest part of a candle.
      • The closeThe only price both sides agreed to end on. Closes decide structure; wicks decide invalidation. Never break structure on a wick.
      • The next candleA rejection candle nobody follows is a curiosity. Confirmation is not superstition here — it is the difference between one participant and a side.

      04

      Structure

      the skeleton under the noise

      Structure is the sequence of swing highs and lows, and it is the only objective thing on a chart. Two traders will disagree about a pattern; they should not disagree about whether the last higher low held.

      • Define a swing once, then never move the definitionA swing high is a high with lower highs either side of it. Pick the number of bars, write it down, apply it every day. Most bad structure reads are a definition that quietly changed to fit the position.
      • Break of structureA close beyond the last swing in the direction of the trend. It is continuation, and it is the weakest of the structural events — the trend was already doing that.
      • Change of characterA close beyond the last opposing swing after an extreme. It is the first evidence that the sequence has stopped. It is permission to look for a reversal, never permission to take one.
      • The fractal problemEvery timeframe has its own structure and they disagree constantly. Fix the roles before you look: the higher timeframe grants direction, the middle one grants location, the low one grants timing. A 5M change of character inside a 4H uptrend is a pullback, not a reversal.

      Reading structure in four moves

      1. Mark the last three swing highs and three swing lows on your bias timeframe. If you cannot mark them cleanly, the market is in balance — stop, and trade the range playbook or nothing.
      2. Name the sequence out loud: higher highs and higher lows, lower highs and lower lows, or neither.
      3. Find the level that would end the sequence. That is your invalidation, and it exists before you have a trade.
      4. Only now drop a timeframe to look for an entry, and only in the direction the sequence allows.
      05

      Location

      the half of the edge nobody photographs

      The same trigger is a good trade at one price and a bad one twenty dollars away. Location is what separates them, and it is decided before the candle prints.

      Premium and discount

      Split the leg you are trading in half. Buying in the lower half is a discount; buying in the upper half is paying a premium for the same idea with a wider stop and a shorter run to target. The maths punishes the second one twice.

      Imbalance

      A three-candle gap where one side never traded. Price returns to it because business was left unfinished there. It is an entry zone on the way back and a target on the way out.

      Origin

      The last opposing candle before a displacement leg — where the size that caused the move was absorbed. Use the body, not the wick. If it fails on the first touch, the premise behind the move has changed.

      Pools

      Equal highs, equal lows, the previous day's extremes, the session range. Not resistance — destinations. Ask which pool price is travelling toward and you have your target before you have your entry.

      The test. Before any entry, answer this in one sentence: why here and not twenty dollars away? If the answer is "because the candle looked good", you have a trigger and no location, which is the most common losing trade there is.
      06

      The read

      six questions, in order

      This is the sequence, and the order is not negotiable. Each step narrows what the next one is allowed to conclude. Work it top to bottom on any chart, in any market, and you will either arrive at a trade with an invalidation or arrive at "nothing here" — both are correct outcomes.

      Work down the list. The verdict appears once all six are answered.

      This is the same order the flow enforces in steps 1 to 4 — condition and structure become the narrative and the router, location and liquidity become the level board, trigger becomes the playbook confirmations, invalidation becomes the stop. The flow is this read, with gates attached.

      Worked example — a London sweep, read in order

      1. Condition. Asia built a tight range on overlapping candles: balance. Value sits in the middle of that range, price is at its lower edge.
      2. Structure. The 4H is making higher highs and higher lows; the last higher low is at 3341. That level ends the sequence, so it is the boundary of everything that follows.
      3. Location. Price is in the lower half of the leg from 3341 to 3372 — a discount, and it is sitting on the Asia low rather than in mid-range.
      4. Liquidity. Equal lows sit just under the Asia low; the previous day high is untouched above. Stops below, destination above. The pool beneath is what the move needs to fund itself.
      5. Trigger. Written in advance: a wick through the equal lows that closes back inside, then a 5M close above the last lower high, then a displacement leg that leaves a gap.
      6. Invalidation. A close back under 3341. That would mean the sweep was not a sweep but continuation, and that the sellers were never trapped.
      What makes it a read rather than a guess. Every one of those six sentences was true before the entry candle existed. If you can only write them afterwards, you are describing, not reading.
      07

      How reads fail

      the six ways, in order of cost

      Almost every losing trade that followed a rule was one of these. They are worth more attention than any pattern, because they are the failures you can actually stop repeating.

      • Trigger without locationA textbook candle in the middle of a range. The pattern was real; there was no reason for it to be at that price and no level to be wrong against. This is the most expensive habit in retail trading.
      • Structure read on the wrong timeframeFading a 4H uptrend because the 5M made a lower low. The 5M is allowed to disagree — that is what a pullback is.
      • Mistaking a gap for strengthA vertical leg into thin liquidity looks like conviction and is often just an empty book at a quiet hour. Ask what time it is before you call it displacement.
      • Fading absorptionSelling into a level that has held four times, on the fifth test. Repeated holds mean someone is filling — until they pull, and the fifth test is the one that goes.
      • Reading the advertisementTrusting a level because it is obvious. The more obvious it is, the more certain it is to be traded through at least once to collect what is resting beyond it.
      • Narrative driftThe read was written for the session, price did something else, and the story quietly changed to keep the position alive. The invalidation you wrote at step 6 exists to stop exactly this.
      The diagnostic. After any loss, ask which of these six it was before you ask anything about the entry. Five of them happen before the entry candle, and four of them are decided in the first two minutes of your read.

        01

        First principles

        why any of this works

        Market mechanics

        • Price moves when one side is willing to cross the spread. Nothing else moves it
        • Every trade needs a counterparty — to buy size, someone must be persuaded or forced to sell
        • The visible chart is the residue of that negotiation, not the negotiation itself
        • A level matters because resting orders sit there, not because it is drawn on your screenThe line is a proxy for where the orders are.
        • Never assume the market knows your position. It knows where the orders are, and yours is one of them

        Auction theory

        • The market is an auction searching for a price that pauses trade — value
        • It moves away from value to advertise a price, and returns when the advertisement is rejected
        • Acceptance — time spent at a price. Accepted prices become the new value area
        • Rejection — a fast return. Leaves a wick, an imbalance, and trapped participants
        • Balance builds a range; imbalance produces a trend. Every day is one or the otherWhich one it is decides which playbook you are allowed to use.

        Liquidity

        • Liquidity is resting orders — stops, limits, pending entries. It is fuel, not a barrier
        • It pools where price is obvious: prior highs and lows, equal levels, round numbers, trendlines
        • Large orders need it. To fill a big buy you must find sellers, and sellers are clustered below the lows
        • That is the mechanical reason price sweeps a low then reverses — not manipulation, arithmetic
        • Two kinds: external (outside the range — the target) and internal (gaps and blocks inside it — the entry)

        Price action

        • Every candle is a story of who won the period. Body is the outcome, wick is the argument
        • Long wicks mean an attempt was rejected. Repeated wicks at one price mean it is defended
        • Speed carries information: the same distance covered fast means conviction, slow means negotiation
        • Where a candle closes matters far more than where it tradedClose beyond a level is acceptance; a wick through it is a raid.
        • Never read a candle without its location. A hammer at the range low and a hammer mid-range are different events

        Order flow

        • Absorption — price stops moving while volume keeps trading. Someone large is filling passively
        • Initiative — aggressive orders lift offers or hit bids, creating displacement
        • Exhaustion — heavy volume with no progress. The aggressor has run out
        • Institutions slice orders over hours. Real accumulation looks like repeated defence of one level, not one giant candle
        • On spot gold you see this indirectly, through wicks, imbalance and how fast a level gets reclaimed

        Market regimes

        • Trend — imbalance persists, pullbacks are shallow, each swing extends. Join at value
        • Range — balance, two respected edges, mean reversion pays. Fade the edges
        • Transition — compression before expansion, or a failed trend. The most expensive place to hold a strong opinion
        • Shock — news or liquidation. Structure stops describing anything. Stand aside
        • Regime first, pattern second. The same shape means opposite things in a trend and in a rangeThis is why the router is step 2 and not an afterthought.

        Confluence

        how many is enough

        Stacking rules

        • Confluence means factors from different families agreeing — location, liquidity, structure, timingThree moving averages agreeing is one factor, not three.
        • Two independent factors is a setup. Four is rare and usually means you are looking too hard
        • A factor that only appeared after you formed the opinion does not count
        • Never let confluence justify a bad location. Five reasons at the wrong price is still the wrong price

        The four families

        • Location — HTF level, value area, premium or discount, range edge
        • Liquidity — a pool swept, a gap left behind, an obvious level raided
        • Structure — break of the opposing swing, MSS, higher low forming
        • Timing — kill zone, post-print, session open, fix

        Scoring a setup

        • One factor per family, one point each, maximum four
        • Two points is your minimum to trade. Three is a normal good setup
        • Subtract a point for anything working against you — news window, off-session, extended price, HTF disagreement
        • Score before you enter and write it in the journal. Over fifty trades your grades by score are the most useful table you own

        False confluence

        • Indicators derived from the same price series — EMA, MACD, RSI all agreeing is one input restated
        • Patterns nested inside patterns on lower timeframes
        • Levels drawn after the move that "would have worked"
        • Never count the fact that you want the trade as a factor. It is the most common one on the list
